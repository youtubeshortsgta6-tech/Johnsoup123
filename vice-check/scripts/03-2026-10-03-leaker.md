## Chapter: Cold Open

A 17-year-old with a hacked Amazon Fire Stick and a hotel TV cost Rockstar Games five million dollars, and four years later he's still the reason GTA6 fans can't fully trust a "leak" when they see one. He's been in a psychiatric hospital, he's now in a prison cell, and this November, while Rockstar is trying to launch the biggest game in company history, he's walking into a courtroom again. This is the full story of the leaker Rockstar has never fully shut down, and the brand-new leaker who just picked up where he left off.

## Chapter: The Heist That Shouldn't Have Worked

In September 2022, a teenager going by the online handle "teapotuberhacker" pulled off one of gaming's most embarrassing security breaches. His name, later confirmed in court, was Arion Kurtaj. At the time he was seventeen, already out on bail for hacking Nvidia, and living under police supervision at a Travelodge hotel in Bicester, England, with his laptop confiscated as a bail condition — that should have stopped him, but it didn't. Investigators say Kurtaj used a hotel TV, a mobile phone, and an Amazon Fire Stick to get back online, then talked his way into Rockstar's internal Slack channels through social engineering. Once inside, he pulled more than ninety clips of unreleased GTA6 development footage, tried to ransom Rockstar for the material, and when the company refused to pay, dumped the videos publicly on GTAForums under his hacker alias. Rockstar later said the breach cost roughly five million dollars in recovery and security work, on top of thousands of staff hours. For a game that hadn't even had its first trailer yet, that's where the entire GTA6 leak era began.

## Chapter: The Arrest And The Trial That Wasn't Normal

Kurtaj was arrested days after the leak, tied by investigators to the hacking collective Lapsus$, the same group linked to breaches at Nvidia, Uber, Microsoft, and Samsung. But his case never went through a normal criminal trial: court-appointed psychiatrists determined Kurtaj was unfit to stand trial because severe autism made it impossible for him to participate in his own defense, so instead of a guilty-or-not-guilty verdict, a London jury held what's called a "trial of the facts" — essentially deciding whether he committed the acts without formally convicting him of a crime. In December 2023, that jury found he did, and the judge then had to decide what to do with a defendant the court had already ruled couldn't be properly tried in the first place.

## Chapter: An Indefinite Hospital Order

The judge's decision was an indefinite hospital order under the UK Mental Health Act — not a fixed sentence, but open-ended psychiatric detention that would only end when doctors decided Kurtaj no longer posed a danger to the public. On paper, that sounds like the story ends there, quietly, in a secure facility with no release date attached. But his psychiatric assessment revealed something that should worry anyone hoping this was over: evaluators noted that Kurtaj "continued to express the intent to return to cyber-crime as soon as possible," and called him "highly motivated." That's not a rehabilitated hacker. That's a ticking clock, and in 2026, it started moving.

## Chapter: Moved To Prison, Right Before Launch

In July 2026, Kurtaj was transferred out of the secure psychiatric hospital and into a standard UK prison, after medical reassessments concluded his condition had stabilized enough for a full criminal retrial — scheduled for November 2026, the same month GTA6 actually ships. And it gets messier: reporting from March 2026 described Kurtaj using a phone to discuss the stolen GTA6 source code with other users online. When asked directly whether the full build had leaked publicly, his only response was "definitely somewhere," before declining to comment further. That's not a confirmation the source code is circulating, but it's not a denial either — and from someone who's already gotten footage out of a secured system once, it's exactly the kind of ambiguous threat that keeps Rockstar's security team up at night.

## Chapter: The New Leaker, CyberLeek

And here's the part that proves Rockstar still hasn't solved this problem: Kurtaj isn't even the only leaker anymore. In August 2026, a separate, unidentified individual operating under the alias "CyberLeek" began posting gameplay clips online days before Rockstar's own planned "Extended Look" broadcast on Netflix. Unlike Kurtaj's 2022 breach, CyberLeek's leaks reportedly showed generic gameplay with no story spoilers, packaged with a consumer-rights manifesto demanding no digital-only preorders, no on-disc DLC, and mandatory offline modes, even tied to a crypto token that briefly built a seven-figure market cap around the leak itself. As of the most recent reporting, CyberLeek has not been publicly identified or arrested. Two completely different leakers, four years apart, same result: Rockstar scrambling to respond before its own marketing team gets to control the story.

## Chapter: Rockstar's Response, Both Times

Rockstar's public response to both incidents has followed almost the exact same script. In September 2022, the company confirmed the breach directly, saying it was "extremely disappointed" that details of the game had been shared "in this way," while insisting it wouldn't have a long-term effect on development. In August 2026, nearly four years and one entire leaker later, Rockstar's statement used almost identical language, calling the new leak "heartbreaking for our team" and asking fans to "wait a bit longer" for the real thing on November 19th. Same company, same tone, two entirely different hackers — that should worry any GTA6 fan trying to stay spoiler-free before launch day. This isn't a problem Rockstar solved once. It's a pattern.

## Chapter: Close

So here's where it actually stands: the hacker who started this whole saga is out of the hospital and back in a courtroom the same month the game ships, he's hinted the source code is "somewhere" without denying it, and a second, still-unidentified leaker just proved the door isn't fully closed. Two leakers, one launch date, and Rockstar still hasn't found a way to guarantee the next reveal comes from them first.
