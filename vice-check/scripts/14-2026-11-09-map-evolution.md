## Chapter: Cold Open

Twenty-five years ago, Rockstar gave you one city — now, in a few days, they're about to give you the biggest world they've ever built, and if the reported numbers hold up, it isn't close. This is the full size-up: every GTA map, generation by generation, ending with what GTA 6's Leonida is reported to be. Not vibes. Numbers.

## Chapter: GTA III, One City, One Idea

Start at the beginning: October 2001, GTA III drops and does something no open-world game had done at this scale, dropping you into a fully 3D, drivable, shootable city. Liberty City is one city, three islands, connected by bridges you unlock as you play, and by the numbers most commonly cited, that map comes out to around eight square kilometers — about three square miles. Tiny by today's standard, but in 2001 that was the whole open-world genre being invented in real time: one city, one idea, prove the concept works.

## Chapter: Vice City, Same Blueprint, New Skin

A year later, Vice City reuses that same engine and one-city structure, just re-skinned as a Miami-inspired setting with a new tone, soundtrack, and era. Map-wise it's roughly the same size class as GTA III, still the "one dense city" era of the franchise — Rockstar hadn't scaled the world yet, they were scaling the atmosphere.

## Chapter: San Andreas, The Leap To Three Cities

Then in 2004, San Andreas changes the entire ambition of the series: instead of one city, you get an entire state — Los Santos, San Fierro, and Las Venturas, three cities modeled after LA, San Francisco, and Las Vegas, connected by open countryside, mountains, and desert you can drive across. By commonly cited measurements, San Andreas's total map lands around 38 square kilometers, about five times bigger than GTA III's, and that's the first time the series treats the world itself as the pitch, not just the city inside it. It's also the game that proves something Rockstar leans on for the next twenty years — bigger doesn't automatically mean better, but bigger done right creates a sense of a living state instead of a level.

## Chapter: GTA IV, Rockstar Goes Smaller On Purpose

Here's the twist most size-ranking videos skip — GTA IV in 2008 actually shrinks the map back down, returning to a single, dense reimagining of Liberty City instead of San Andreas's three-city sprawl. Why? They rebuilt the engine from scratch for realistic physics, lighting, and NPC behavior, and a denser, smaller map let every block feel hand-crafted instead of empty. It's proof this franchise has never treated "map size" as the only goal — density and detail matter just as much.

## Chapter: GTA V, The Modern Benchmark

Then 2013 happens, and GTA V resets the scale of the entire industry. Los Santos and Blaine County together — city, mountains, desert, and ocean you can dive into — commonly cited measurements put the total map around 76 square kilometers, roughly 30 square miles of fully explorable terrain, before you even count the ocean floor, which Rockstar built out in full detail that almost nobody explores. GTA V has now been re-released across three console generations, sold over 230 million copies, and thirteen years later is still one of the best-selling and most-played games on the planet. This is the map GTA 6 has to beat — not "impress people a little more than." Beat.

## Chapter: GTA 6, What's Actually Reported

So here's where we slow down and get honest about what's confirmed and what isn't. Rockstar hasn't published an official square-mileage figure for GTA 6's map — what we do have is a report from YouTuber TGG, who got an exclusive walkthrough at Rockstar North and relayed studio comparisons from that trip: Leonida's map is roughly twice the size of GTA V's, and roughly three times the size of Red Dead Redemption 2's, with Vice City itself reportedly around twice the size of Los Santos, and Vice City plus its surrounding suburbs reportedly around eleven times bigger than Los Santos and its immediate surroundings. Say it plainly: this is reported information from a studio visit, not an official Rockstar spec sheet, so treat "twice the size of GTA V" as the current best reporting, not a confirmed number, until Rockstar says so. Even with that caveat, the pattern across twenty-five years holds: one city, three cities, a smaller deliberate reset, then the biggest single map the franchise has built — and now reportedly, the biggest jump yet.

## Chapter: Why This Actually Matters

Here's why the scale story isn't just trivia — every time Rockstar has jumped map size, it's paired with a jump in what you can actually do in that space: San Andreas added three cities' worth of culture and customization, GTA V added three switchable protagonists and a heist structure built around traveling between locations. If Leonida is reportedly double GTA V's footprint, the design question isn't "is it big," it's what Rockstar builds into that space to make it worth driving through — and from what's been shown in official trailers so far, wildlife, weather, and a living swamp ecosystem are clearly part of that answer.

## Chapter: Close

Twenty-five years, five mainline maps, one clear pattern — bigger, then smarter, then biggest — and when Leonida ships on November 19th, we'll finally get the real number instead of the reported one.
