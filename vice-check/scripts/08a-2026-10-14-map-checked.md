## Chapter: Cold Open

[VISUAL: Trailer 2 aerial over the Vice City skyline, then the Trailer 1 Keys bridges. Hold two seconds before the voice.]

Type "GTA 6 map" into YouTube and you'll get a hundred videos, a dozen fan-made maps, and one number repeated everywhere: twice the size of GTA 5. Here's the problem. Rockstar has never said that. So today we're doing the whole map, region by region, and sorting every claim into three piles. Confirmed by Rockstar. Reported by someone who was in the room. Or made up on a forum. By the end you'll know exactly how much of the map you've seen is real.

## Chapter: What Rockstar Has Actually Confirmed

[VISUAL: original Leonida outline graphic, six region names appearing one by one, labeled "approximate positions."]

Start with the short list, because it is short. The game is set in the state of Leonida, Rockstar's take on Florida, and Rockstar's own site names six regions. Vice City. The Leonida Keys. The Grassrivers. Port Gellhorn. Ambrosia. And Mount Kalaga National Park. It's one continuous map, no loading between regions, and Rockstar calls it their biggest world yet. That's it. That's the confirmed list. No square mileage. No official map image. No coordinates. Everything past this point is either reporting or guesswork, and I'll tell you which.

## Chapter: The Size Number, Checked

[VISUAL: card with three bars: GTA V (76 km², community measurement), RDR2, Leonida (reported). Label "reported" in a different color.]

Now the number everyone quotes. The source is a YouTuber, TGG, who was given a walkthrough at Rockstar North and relayed what the studio compared the map to. Roughly twice the size of GTA 5. Roughly three times Red Dead Redemption 2. Vice City itself about twice the size of Los Santos. That's a real report from a real visit, and it's the best information anyone has. It is not a spec sheet. Rockstar has never published a figure, and the studio comparisons were spoken, not measured.

So let's do the arithmetic honestly. Community measurements put GTA 5's map at around seventy-six square kilometers. Double that and you're near a hundred and fifty. If the report holds, Leonida would be about the size of a real mid-sized American city's whole metro footprint. But the moment you see a video with an exact number, like a hundred and fifty-two point three square kilometers, that's someone doing math on a rumor and presenting it as a measurement. File it under reported, not confirmed.

## Chapter: The Fan Maps, Checked

[VISUAL: original graphic, a blurred silhouette of a "fan map" shape with a big question mark. Do not show any actual fan map or leaked layout.]

Now the fan maps. There are two kinds, and it matters which one you're looking at. The first kind is built from official material only: trailer frames, screenshots, the signs and road markers Rockstar has shown. Those maps are honest guesses. They get the rough shape right, because Rockstar has shown enough coastline and enough skyline to place the big pieces. The second kind is built on the 2022 leak. Those maps come with coordinates, road grids and district names pulled from stolen development files. We don't show that material, and here's the thing most people miss: that leak was a four-year-old build. Rockstar changes maps right up to shipping. A road that existed in 2022 may not exist now.

So the verdict on fan maps is simple. The outline, probably close. The scale, unknown. The details, unverifiable until November 19th. Keep that in mind and the rest of this video will make sense.

## Chapter: South: The Leonida Keys

[VISUAL: Trailer 1 aerial of the bridges, then the whale statue screenshot.]

Now the tour, south to north. The bottom of the map is the Leonida Keys, and this is the most confirmed region on the whole map, because Rockstar has shown so much of it. Long bridges over open water, stilt houses, boats everywhere. Rockstar's own line is that the dress code is casual and the bars are loaded. It's also where the story starts for Jason. His official background puts him in the Keys after the Army, working for local drug runners, living rent-free in a property owned by a smuggler named Brian Heder. So confirmed: the Keys exist, they're a chain of islands linked by road, and they're a story hub. Unconfirmed: how many islands, and how far the chain runs.

## Chapter: The Grassrivers and Lake Leonida

[VISUAL: official Grassrivers screenshots, airboat and gators, then the Ambrosia refinery.]

North of the Keys, the Grassrivers. This is the Everglades, and Rockstar built an entire swamp region with its own vehicles, airboats included. Rockstar's description says the gators are the famous attraction but they're not the deadliest thing out there. Confirmed: it's a real region with its own ecosystem, not a strip of swamp between two cities.

In the middle of the state sits Lake Leonida, the big lake, and on its shore, Ambrosia. That's the sugar town. Rockstar says the refinery, Allied Crystal, provides the jobs and the local biker gang provides almost everything else. Official images even show the sugar company's name on election yard signs. Confirmed: a lake big enough to anchor the middle of the map, and a working town on it. That's a real answer to a real question, because GTA 5's middle was mostly empty desert. This map's middle has a lake and a town.

## Chapter: West and North: Port Gellhorn and Mount Kalaga

[VISUAL: Port Gellhorn postcard, Starlet Motel, then the Mount Kalaga forest and bluffs.]

On the west coast, Port Gellhorn. Rockstar calls it Leonida's forgotten coast. Cheap motels, shut attractions, empty strip malls. The liquor store from the first trailer, Uncle Jack's, is here. Confirmed: a second coast, which means the map has water on more than one side. Most fan maps agree on this, and for once they're on solid ground, because Rockstar has said it directly.

At the top, Mount Kalaga National Park. Forests, rivers, rock bluffs. Rockstar says the backwoods are home to hillbilly mystics and paranoid radicals. And here's the one honest gap in the whole map: Florida doesn't have mountains. Its highest natural point is three hundred forty-five feet. So whatever Mount Kalaga is, it's Rockstar's invention, and nobody outside Rockstar knows how tall or how big it is. Any fan map that draws a mountain range up there is drawing from imagination.

## Chapter: Vice City, The Part You've Actually Seen

[VISUAL: Trailer 2 skyline, then rapid official shots: Ocean Beach, murals, the arena, the port.]

[PAUSE]

And then the city. Vice City is where Rockstar has shown the most, and where the fan maps are most reliable, because the trailers give you real landmarks to pin. Rockstar's site names the districts. Ocean Beach, the art deco hotels on white sand. Little Cuba. The Tisha-Wocka flea market. A port Rockstar calls the cruise ship capital of the world. Add the arena, the Sahara Arena, home of the Vice City Narcos, and the street-art district you see in Trailer 2. Those are confirmed places with confirmed names. The reported part is the scale: about twice Los Santos for the city, and with the suburbs, around eleven times the Los Santos surroundings. Again, that's from the studio visit, not a spec sheet.

Here's the check that matters. Every district Rockstar has named lines up with a real Miami neighborhood, which means the city's layout almost certainly follows Miami's real geography: a mainland city, a causeway, and a beach on a barrier island. If a fan map puts the beach somewhere else, the fan map is wrong.

## Chapter: What Twice GTA 5 Would Feel Like

[VISUAL: original graphic, GTA 5's map outline next to a same-scale blank shape twice its area. Then the Extended Look night skyline and the rooftop pool shot.]

Here's a way to make the reported number mean something. GTA 5's map, Los Santos plus Blaine County, takes about six or seven minutes to drive top to bottom on the freeway at full speed, and that's a map most people never finished exploring. Double the area and you're looking at a drive from the Keys to Mount Kalaga that runs closer to a quarter of an hour, with a swamp, a lake, a sugar town and a second coast in between. That's the difference between a map you cross and a map you travel.

And the Extended Look from August gave the first real sense of that density. Vice City at night from a rooftop, lit to the horizon. A pool deck high above the beach. Weather rolling in over the skyline. Rockstar hasn't shown the whole map, but it has shown that the parts it built are built all the way down. So when someone asks whether twice the size means twice as empty, the official footage so far says no. The reported size is unconfirmed. The detail is on screen.

## Chapter: What Nobody Knows Yet

[VISUAL: card listing the open questions, one appearing per line.]

So what's genuinely unknown, thirty-six days out? The total size. The number of islands. Whether the whole map is open from the first minute, the way GTA 5 was, or unlocks as you go, the way GTA 3 did. How much of Mount Kalaga you can actually climb. And whether there's anything past the edges, like the Cayo Perico style island fans keep predicting. None of that has been confirmed, and anyone selling you an answer is selling you a guess.

## Chapter: The Scoreboard

[VISUAL: the Leonida outline again. Six regions light up green, "SIZE" stays amber with "reported." End screen from the final eight seconds.]

Here's the scoreboard. Six regions, confirmed. Two coasts, confirmed. A lake and a town in the middle, confirmed. A city built on Miami's real geography, confirmed by every named district. The size, twice GTA 5, reported by one credible source and never confirmed. And the fan maps, right about the shape, unproven about everything else. On November 19th we measure it ourselves.
