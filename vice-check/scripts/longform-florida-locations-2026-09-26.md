## Chapter: The lobster clue

[VISUAL: slow push-in on the official Leonida Keys screenshot showing the giant whale statue. Hard cut to a real photo of Betsy the Lobster.]

Rockstar's official GTA 6 site never once uses the word Florida. But one official screenshot shows a giant roadside statue that's a straight parody of a real thirty-foot lobster in the Florida Keys. And that's not even the strangest match on this map.

## Chapter: Leonida's six regions

[VISUAL: VICE Check title card. Then a simple original graphic of Leonida's outline, with six region names appearing one by one. Label it "approximate positions."]

Grand Theft Auto 6 is set in Leonida. A fictional state. Rockstar has named six regions. Vice City. The Leonida Keys. The Grassrivers. Port Gellhorn. Ambrosia. And Mount Kalaga National Park.

A lot of people say it's just Florida with a fake name slapped on top. They're half right. Five of those six regions line up with a real place. Some are obvious. One is a small sugar town most GTA fans have never heard of. And one region doesn't have a clean real-world match at all.

We're going south to north, and the last stop is Vice City, because that's where Rockstar hid the most. Everything here comes from official trailers, screenshots, and Rockstar's own site. No leaked footage. And when a match is a fan theory, I'll tell you.

## Chapter: Leonida Keys

[VISUAL: Trailer 1 aerial of the bridges over turquoise water. Split screen with a licensed or Creative Commons photo of the Overseas Highway.]

First stop, the Leonida Keys. The long bridges over open water are the giveaway. That's the Overseas Highway, the road that strings the real Florida Keys together all the way down to Key West.

Rockstar pitches the Keys as the easy life. Their own line: the dress code is casual, the bars are loaded. Stilt houses. Boats everywhere. And it matters for the story. Jason's official background puts him right here. After the Army, he ended up working for local drug runners in the Keys. Rockstar also introduces Brian Heder, a veteran Keys smuggler, and Jason lives in one of Brian's properties rent-free. The catch? He helps with local shakedowns.

[VISUAL: back to the whale screenshot, then side by side with a Betsy photo.]

Now, the lobster. In Islamorada, there's a fiberglass lobster named Betsy. Thirty feet tall. Forty feet long. Finished in 1985. It's the second most photographed attraction in the entire Keys. In GTA 6, a roadside tourist stop that looks almost identical shows up in official images. Same setup. Except Betsy has been swapped for a giant whale.

That's Rockstar's whole method in one shot. Take a real place. Change one detail. Call it somewhere else. So if the Keys are paradise, the next region is the opposite.

## Chapter: Grassrivers

[VISUAL: official Grassrivers screenshots: airboat, mangroves, gator.]

The Grassrivers. This is the Everglades, and Rockstar isn't being subtle about it. Sawgrass. Mangroves. Slow brown water. Airboats. Rockstar's own description says the gators are the famous attraction, but they're not the deadliest thing out there.

The real Everglades National Park covers about one and a half million acres. That's the scale Rockstar is borrowing from. And here's why this matters. The original Vice City in 2002 never left the city limits. Two islands, one city, done. This time Rockstar built an entire swamp region, with vehicles made for it.

[VISUAL: Trailer 1 alligator-in-pool shot. No real viral clip on screen; describe it only.]

Rockstar studied more than the geography, too. The first trailer is packed with moments that echo real Florida viral videos. The gator in the pool? Florida has produced that exact headline more than once. From the swamp, head north to the big lake. This is the region nobody talks about.

## Chapter: Ambrosia

[VISUAL: official Ambrosia postcard and refinery screenshots. Map graphic zooms to Lake Leonida.]

Ambrosia. It sits on the shore of Lake Leonida, the huge lake in the middle of the state. In real Florida, that's Lake Okeechobee. It's the biggest freshwater lake in the state, about seven hundred thirty square miles. Big enough to anchor the middle of the whole map. And on its southwest shore sits a town called Clewiston.

Clewiston calls itself America's Sweetest Town. The whole place runs on sugar. Cane fields, a sugar mill, rail lines cutting through town.

Now look at Ambrosia. It's anchored by a massive sugar refinery called Allied Crystal. Rockstar's own description says the refinery provides the jobs, and the local biker gang provides almost everything else. Even the name is a joke. Ambrosia is the food of the gods. Something impossibly sweet.

[VISUAL: slow zoom on the official image showing an Allied Crystal election sign.]

And official images show Allied Crystal branding on local election signs. A sugar company backing a mayor's race. That's not a random detail. That's Rockstar telling you exactly who runs this town. Most GTA channels skip Ambrosia completely. It might be the most carefully researched region on the whole map.

## Chapter: Port Gellhorn

[VISUAL: official Port Gellhorn postcard: Starlet Motel, strip malls, dirt bikes.]

Now cross to the west coast. Port Gellhorn. Rockstar calls it Leonida's forgotten coast. Cheap motels like the Starlet. Shut-down attractions. Empty strip malls. And my favorite line on Rockstar's whole site: the new economy here is fueled by malt liquor, painkillers, and truck stop energy drinks.

The best match is Panama City, on Florida's Gulf coast. Panama City Beach used to be one of the biggest spring break spots in America, until the city cracked down on it in 2015. And here's a fan theory I think is right. Martha Gellhorn was a legendary war correspondent. Late in her career, she reported on the 1989 US invasion of Panama. Gellhorn. Panama. Panama City. Rockstar hasn't confirmed it, but that's a lot of coincidence. Some fans think the Port part comes from Port Charlotte, further south. Either way, it's Gulf coast Florida.

[VISUAL: Trailer 1 liquor store robbery shot, Uncle Jack's Liquor sign visible. Cut before any weapon close-up.]

And the first trailer already took us here. That liquor store robbery? That's Uncle Jack's Liquor, in Port Gellhorn.

## Chapter: Mount Kalaga

[VISUAL: official Mount Kalaga screenshots: forest, river, bluffs.]

Head north one more time. Mount Kalaga National Park. Forests. Rivers. Rock bluffs. Rockstar says the backwoods are home to hillbilly mystics and paranoid radicals, living far from the prying eyes of the government.

And this is the one region with no clean real-world match. Here's the problem. Florida doesn't really have mountains. Its highest natural point, Britton Hill, is only three hundred forty-five feet above sea level. So the forests and rivers feel like north Florida, but the mountain itself is Rockstar's invention. The closest thing is Torreya State Park, where tall bluffs rise over the Apalachicola River. But calling that a mountain is a stretch.

If you've got a theory on what inspired Mount Kalaga, drop it in the comments. I genuinely haven't seen a convincing one yet.

## Chapter: Vice City

[VISUAL: best Vice City skyline shot from Trailer 2. Let it breathe for two seconds before the voiceover.]

[PAUSE]

Remember the promise at the start? Vice City is where Rockstar hid the most. And finally, here it is. Rockstar's site names the key spots. Ocean Beach, with pastel art deco hotels and white sand. Little Cuba. The Tisha-Wocka flea market. And a port Rockstar calls the cruise ship capital of the world.

Match them up. Ocean Beach is Miami Beach and South Beach. Little Cuba is Little Havana. Tisha-Wocka? Say it out loud. It rhymes with Opa-locka, a real city just north of Miami, home to one of the biggest indoor flea markets in the area. Rockstar hasn't confirmed it, but fans matched that one fast. And PortMiami really does bill itself as the cruise capital of the world.

[VISUAL: rapid side-by-sides, about 3 seconds each. Game shot on the left, real photo on the right.]

Now the fast ones. The Ocean View Hotel. Same sign style as the real Clevelander on Ocean Drive. The Sahara Arena, home of the Vice City Narcos. That building's shape is the Kaseya Center, where the Miami Heat play. Club NINE1NINE. Miami's most famous around-the-clock club is called E11EVEN. Nine-one-nine. Eleven-eleven. The street murals in Trailer 2? That's Wynwood, Miami's street-art district. The causeway from the city out to the beach is the Venetian Causeway. And fans even matched a penthouse pool from the first trailer to a real building in Miami Beach, the Tresor Tower.

So, is Leonida just Florida with a fake name? No. It's Florida run through Rockstar's filter. Every real place gets one twist. A lobster becomes a whale. A sugar town gets a biker gang. A war reporter's name becomes a city.
