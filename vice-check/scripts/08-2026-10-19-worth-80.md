## Chapter: Cold Open

"Eighty dollars for a video game is insane." I've seen that comment a thousand times since Rockstar confirmed GTA6's price, and I get the instinct — but when you actually run the numbers instead of reacting to the headline, the "not worth it" argument falls apart fast. Today I'm making the real economic case for why eighty dollars is one of the best entertainment deals you'll find in 2026, and I'm steelmanning the other side first, because that's the fair way to do this.

## Chapter: Steelmanning The Not Worth It Case

Let's be fair to the criticism first, because it's not baseless. People are dealing with real cost-of-living pressure right now, and eighty dollars up front — plus another twenty for Ultimate — is a genuine chunk of money for a lot of households. There's also the physical-copy issue: GTA6's boxed copies come with a download code instead of a disc, which frustrated fans who felt like they weren't getting a tangible product for that price. And there's a fair point that seventy-to-eighty-dollar pricing, if it becomes the industry norm, could squeeze out smaller studios that can't justify it without AAA-level marketing. Those are legitimate concerns, not stupid ones — but none of them actually answer whether GTA6 specifically, at this price, is a bad deal. That's a different question, and it's the one we're answering now.

## Chapter: What Seventy Becoming Eighty Actually Means

Start with the history, because most people arguing about this have no idea what the actual pattern looks like. Games sat around fifty to sixty dollars through most of the 2000s and 2010s, then jumped to seventy dollars for AAA titles around 2020 with the new console generation — reported at the time as the first major industry-wide price increase in about fifteen years. Adjust historical game prices for inflation, and a sixty-dollar game from the 2010s actually works out to somewhere around sixty to seventy of today's dollars. So the jump from seventy to eighty isn't some unprecedented money grab, it's roughly in line with where inflation alone would have put the number anyway. And GTA6 isn't even the game that set the eighty-dollar bar — Nintendo's Mario Kart World already launched at that price last year. GTA6 is just the release big enough that people finally noticed the number.

## Chapter: The Budget Nobody's Talking About

Here's the number that actually explains the price better than any meme does. GTA6 has been widely reported as the most expensive video game ever made, with a reported development budget in the range of three billion dollars. Take-Two has never confirmed that number, so treat it as an estimate. For context, Red Dead Redemption 2 — Rockstar's own previous flagship, one of the most acclaimed open-world games ever released — cost somewhere between three hundred seventy and five hundred forty million dollars to make. That puts GTA6's budget at roughly twenty times the game a lot of people already consider the gold standard for production value, and you don't get that level of investment back by pricing it the same as it was ten years ago. Eighty dollars isn't Rockstar being greedy, it's Rockstar pricing a fundamentally different scale of product.

## Chapter: The Rockstar Track Record

Now let's talk about what you're actually buying into, because Rockstar has receipts here that almost no other publisher can match. GTA5 has sold over 230 million copies worldwide, per Take-Two's own earnings this May, and it's still climbing over a decade after release, and it's been reported as one of the most-played games on PlayStation years and years after launch — which tells you everything about how long a Rockstar open world stays relevant. That's not a game people finished once and shelved, it's one people are still logging hours in more than a decade later, across three console generations. If GTA6 delivers anywhere close to that staying power, the actual cost-per-hour, spread out over years of play, ends up lower than almost anything else you could spend eighty dollars on in entertainment.

## Chapter: What Else Eighty Dollars Buys

Let's put eighty dollars in perspective against other things people spend money on without blinking. A single concert ticket can run well past a hundred dollars for a few hours of entertainment, a month of multiple streaming subscriptions stacked together easily clears thirty to forty dollars for content you don't even own and that disappears the moment you cancel, and a night out at the movies for two, with snacks, gets you uncomfortably close to eighty dollars for roughly two hours. GTA5 kept people playing for over a decade — if GTA6 gets anywhere near that, we're not talking about an expensive purchase, we're talking about one of the cheapest hours-of-entertainment ratios available anywhere in media in 2026.

## Chapter: Why The Backlash Is Loud But Thin

Here's the pattern worth calling out directly — a lot of the loudest "not worth it" content isn't actually engaging with any of this. It's reaction-format content built for clicks, using GTA6's price as a convenient symbol for cost-of-living frustration that has nothing to do with the game itself, and that doesn't make the frustration fake, it makes it misdirected. GTA6 didn't cause rent to go up, it didn't cause grocery prices to climb — it's an easy target because it's the biggest headline in gaming this year, not because it's actually the worst value proposition in entertainment. Separate "I'm frustrated about money in general" from "this specific product is a bad deal," and those are two very different arguments — only one of them is actually about the game.

## Chapter: The Actual Verdict

So here's where that leaves us. Eighty dollars is roughly where inflation alone would have put AAA pricing anyway, it's backing what's reported to be a three-billion-dollar production — the most expensive game ever made — and it's coming from a studio whose last open world is still selling and still being played over a decade later, with an upgrade path if you want more later instead of committing everything up front. Stack that against a concert ticket or a few streaming subscriptions, and the "not worth it" argument isn't really an economic argument, it's a vibes-based reaction to a big number. The actual math says otherwise.

## Chapter: Close

Now that you've seen the actual numbers — genuinely, is there a game you've paid full price for that gave you more hours than you expected? Tell me which one, because that's exactly the bet people are making on GTA6 right now.
