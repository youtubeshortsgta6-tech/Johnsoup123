# VICE Check: direction after launch

Written 2026-09-28, the night the first video went live. Revisit with real numbers after video 10 (Oct 26) and again the week after launch.

## What the channel is
A faceless fact-check channel for GTA 6. Every video takes a claim people are arguing about, puts the source next to it, and lands on a verdict. The name is the format. That stays.

## Pocket (the cluster to dominate for the next 20 to 30 videos)
"What is actually true about GTA 6", verified against Rockstar's own words before launch and against the real game after launch.
Not: news recaps, reaction, leaks, or Let's Play.

## Gameplay: as evidence, not as content
Do not become a gameplay channel. Let's Plays are personality-driven, saturated on day one, and the pipeline is built for narration over footage.
Do capture your own gameplay from Nov 19 and use it as the footage under every post-launch video. It is owner-licensed, it is what the audience wants to see, and the official press footage runs out the day the game ships. The narration checks the claim; the capture is the proof.

## Cadence
Now to Nov 16: the fifteen scheduled videos, two a week (Phase 1: 2 to 3 per week, get reps in).
Launch week Nov 19 to 25: one a day for five days, short, each answering one question people search that day.
After: three a week through January while the game is new, then settle at two.

## Post-launch slate (draft topics, in order)
Launch week
1. Install size, pre-load, day-one patch: what actually shipped (Nov 19)
2. The map, measured: is Leonida really twice GTA V (Nov 20)
3. Ultimate Edition contents verified item by item: was $99.99 worth it (Nov 21)
4. PS5 vs Series X vs Series S: performance, load times, modes, checked (Nov 22)
5. Every trailer shot found in the game, and the ones that aren't there (Nov 23)
Weeks 2 to 6
6. Real Florida locations, round two: in-game vs real photo, the Miami set
7. Every 2022 leak scored against the shipped game
8. The reviews' biggest claims, checked
9. Things the trailers hid: what Rockstar didn't show and why
10. In-game prices vs real Florida prices
11. Rumored Easter eggs: confirmed, busted, unclear
12. The story timeline explained, spoiler-flagged
13. What the day-one patch changed, from the patch notes
14. GTA 6 vs GTA 5 side by side: the same scenes, twelve years apart
15. Online mode: what's confirmed, what's rumor (ongoing series once Rockstar speaks)
Evergreen series to keep alive
- "Patch notes, explained" on every update
- "Is this real?" on every viral clip or rumor that crosses the shorts channel

## Length
Post-launch scripts target 8:30 to 10:00 so every video carries mid-rolls. The pre-launch batch was mostly 4 to 6 minutes; that was the cost of speed, not a template.

## Data checkpoints (don't change strategy without these)
After video 10: CTR by video, average view duration, which titles broke above the channel average. That tells us which claim-types the audience wants checked.
Launch week: same read. The wave lifts everything, so compare videos to each other, not to September.
Rules: no pivot before 30 videos. A single breakout is a topic signal, not a pivot signal.

## Money
Foundation: AdSense once 1,000 subs and 4,000 watch hours are in (Shorts hours don't count; the long-form channel is the one that earns them).
Second layer that fits this format exactly: affiliate links on the things the videos already check. Controller, SSD for the install, the editions. Amazon Associates first.
Later: sponsorship once the channel clears 50K subs. The fact-check position is what sponsors in this niche pay for.

## Cross-promotion
The GTA6 Shorts channel is the top of the funnel. Every short that touches a claim links to the long-form check. Every long-form description links the shorts channel. Playlist "GTA 6, checked" in posting order on the long-form channel, linked from every description.
